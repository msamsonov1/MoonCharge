Lunar Polar Heliokinetic Coupling & DEM Inversion Physics

At the lunar South Pole (e.g., Shackleton, Malapert Mountain, Connecting Ridge), the solar elevation angle $\theta_\odot$ ranges from merely $1.5^\circ$ to $3.0^\circ$ above the local horizon. The rover's forward traverse velocity vector $\mathbf{v} = [v_x, v_y, v_z]^T$ acts as a spatial-to-temporal transformation frequency operator over sub-meter topographic relief, converting subtle topographic boulders and ridges into instantaneous step-function power dropouts.

1. Dynamic Solar Harvesting & Flux Transfer

Total instantaneous electrical power $E(t)$ collected by the dual-junction InGaP/InGaAs/Ge vertical array depends upon the topographically obstructed solar irradiance $S_0$, array surface area $A_{\text{eff}}$, conversion efficiency $\eta(T)$, and the geometric dot product between array normal vector $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{array}}$ and the solar vector $\hat{\mathbf{s}}$:

$$E(t, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{v}) = \chi_{\text{DEM}}(\mathbf{r}(t), \hat{\mathbf{s}}) \cdot \eta(T) A_{\text{eff}} S_0 \left[ \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{array}}(t) \cdot \hat{\mathbf{s}} \right] - P_{\text{drive}}(\mathbf{v}) - P_{\text{gimbal}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_g)$$

Where $\chi_{\text{DEM}} \in \{0, 1\}$ is the non-linear binary line-of-sight ray function intersecting high-resolution Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) LOLA digital elevation model surfaces:

$$\chi_{\text{DEM}}(\mathbf{r}, \hat{\mathbf{s}}) = \prod_{k=1}^K \mathcal{H}\left( \tan \theta_\odot - \frac{z_{\text{DEM}}(\mathbf{r} + k \Delta \ell \cdot \hat{\mathbf{s}}_{\parallel}) - z(\mathbf{r})}{k \Delta \ell} \right)$$
$\mathcal{H}(\cdot)$ is the Heaviside step function. If any terrain point $k\Delta \ell$ intercepts the grazing low ray, $\chi_{\text{DEM}} = 0$ (instant zero irradiance).

2. $(v, E)$ Spatial-to-Temporal Derivative

As the rover travels with forward velocity $\mathbf{v} = \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}$, the rate of change of harvested energy exhibits an acute sensitivity gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{r}} E$. When traversing near shadow boundary penumbras, the spatial gradient approaches infinity:

$$\frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}\Big|_{\text{ephem}} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla_{\mathbf{r}} \left( \chi_{\text{DEM}} \cdot \Phi_{\text{solar}} \right) - \left( m_{\text{lrv}} v \frac{dv}{dt} + C_{\text{rr}} m_{\text{lrv}} g_{\text{moon}} v \right)$$

A rapid forward drive velocity into a crater depression exponentially sharpens power starvation. Conversely, accelerating onto an elevated crest increases both solar elevation margin and kinetic recovery headroom:

$$\text{Condition for Survival: } \int_0^{\Delta t} \left[ E(t, \mathbf{v}) - P_{\text{avionics}} \right] dt \ge \Delta E_{\text{batt, reserve}}$$
At $v = 0.5\,\text{m/s}$, a $1.5^\circ$ lunar obstacle at 20 m range casts a 760 m shadow cone, demanding automated path re-planning within $\le 3.8\,\text{seconds}$.

3. 40Hz Real-Time Ridge Inversion Observer

The MoonCharge autonomy kernel continuously compares real-time current sensor outputs against the forward-projected DEM horizon vector. If the measured energy gradient deviates from nominal by more than threshold $\epsilon_{\text{shadow}}$, an optimal heading diversion vector $\Delta \psi^*$ is computed:

$$\Delta \psi^* = \arg \max_{\Delta \psi} \int_{0}^{T_{\text{lookahead}}} \left[ E(\mathbf{v}(\psi), \chi_{\text{DEM}}) - \lambda_{\text{slip}} \|\nabla z(\mathbf{r})\|^2 \right] dt$$

Subject to kinematic rover non-holonomic turning and maximum safe slope rollover constraints:

$$\kappa_{\text{turn}} = \left| \frac{d\psi}{ds} \right| \le \kappa_{\text{max}} = \frac{\tan \delta_{\text{steer, max}}}{L_{\text{wheelbase}}}, \quad \|\nabla z(\mathbf{r})\| \le 18.5^\circ$$
The Lyapunov stability function $V(e) = \frac{1}{2} e_E^2 + \frac{1}{2} e_\psi^2$ guarantees exponential asymptotic convergence to maximum-insolation ridgelines.
